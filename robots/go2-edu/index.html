    Unitree Go2 EDU¶

    The Unitree Go2 EDU is a quadruped robot. Prepare the test area and control device before switching it on: startup can involve the robot standing up.

    The startup instructions below follow Unitree's manual. We do not yet have a tested lab startup procedure; the proposed lab routine is a draft to try with the robot owner and then record here.

    Unitree's ROS 2 tooling communicates with Go2 through SDK2 and Cyclone DDS. The upstream project recommends Ubuntu 22.04 with ROS 2 Humble and includes state, standing, and sport-mode examples for Go2. Unitree ROS 2 support

    Before first use¶

    One person controls motion

    Do not run a Unitree movement example, teleoperation program, or custom control node while somebody else has the app or handheld controller active. Pick one control path for each session. Keep the controller or the designated safe-stop control with the operator, not on a desk.

    Startup - Unitree procedure¶

    Source: Unitree's Go2 User Manual, Instructions for use, printed pages 9-10 (PDF pages 7-8): battery installation and body placement and startup. Unitree also publishes the manual on its own website.

    1. Check and fit the battery¶

    Check the parts are sound and the camera and LiDAR are clean and unobstructed. With Go2 on flat ground, insert the battery from the side with its power button facing upwards. It should click into place; if it does not fit, check the orientation rather than forcing it.

    2. Set the starting posture¶

    Place Go2 lying level, with its belly support pad flat on the ground. Fully fold the lower legs; the four knees and feet should rest on the ground, with no leg trapped underneath the body. Check the manual's placement diagram before power-on and keep hands clear of the joints.

    3. Switch on and wait¶

    1. Briefly press the battery power button once.
    2. Press it again and hold for at least two seconds.
    3. Allow about two minutes for startup. The head indicator flashes green while starting and becomes steady green when ready, with the body level.

    Allow room for startup movement

    Keep the area clear throughout power-on. Go2 may stand during startup; do not hold or reposition its legs while they are powered.

    4. Connect the app or controller¶

    First-time app binding and optional handheld-controller pairing are separate setup tasks. Follow Unitree's app instructions (printed pages 11-12) and handheld-controller manual. For subsequent sessions, reconnect the existing device/account; the lab's choice is still to be recorded in the table above.

    The lab network is Robotics Wi-Fi: Go2 should be connected to Robotics-2.4Ghz or Robotics-5Ghz. Use the same lab network on the device running the app when connecting through Wi-Fi.

    Proposed lab startup routine¶

    Draft - not yet tested on the lab Go2

    This is a proposed session workflow, not an established lab procedure. Try it with the robot owner, confirm the controller and stop controls on our robot, then replace the TBC entries above with the tested values.

    1. Record the operator, date, planned experiment, and battery charge.
    2. Set aside a clear, level test area, disconnect charging leads, and choose one person to operate the app or controller. Have an observer for the first movement test.
    3. Confirm the operator knows the stop method for that control device, then complete the Unitree startup steps above.
    4. Reconnect the chosen controller/app and check robot status. For a ROS 2 experiment, follow the laptop checks below after confirming Go2 and your laptop are connected to Robotics Wi-Fi and your laptop's workspace is configured.
    5. Before starting the experiment, try a short, slow movement and a normal stop in the clear area. Record faults and finish with the switching-off procedure.

    To make this the lab procedure, record the exact controller model, account owner, connection steps, stop button sequence, and test-area location. Record your laptop's ROS 2 workspace, network interface, and environment script separately when your development setup is ready. Physical startup can be completed with the app/controller before connecting a laptop.

    Connect your laptop for ROS 2¶

    Researchers use their own laptops for robot development. Set up ROS 2 and the Unitree packages on your laptop using the upstream installation instructions, then join Robotics-2.4Ghz or Robotics-5Ghz. Confirm Go2 is also connected to Robotics Wi-Fi. Its IP address or hostname still needs to be recorded above.

    Run the following in your laptop's ROS 2 terminal. These example paths assume your Unitree workspace is at ~/unitree_ros2; use your actual workspace path if it is elsewhere.

    Go2 ROS 2 environment - read-only checks
    source /opt/ros/humble/setup.bash
source ~/unitree_ros2/cyclonedds_ws/install/setup.bash

# Configure this script with your laptop's Robotics Wi-Fi interface.
source ~/unitree_ros2/setup.sh

# Safe checks: discover available ROS 2 interfaces and inspect motion state.
ros2 topic list
ros2 topic echo /sportmodestate --once

    The upstream setup.sh selects the network interface used by Cyclone DDS; the interface name can differ between researchers' laptops. If you see no Go2 topics, first confirm both devices are connected to Robotics Wi-Fi and that your script selects your laptop's Wi-Fi interface. Ask the robot owner for help if communication still fails. Unitree network setup

    Common modules¶

    Read robot state¶

    Run these checks from the ROS 2 terminal on your laptop:

    ros2 topic list
ros2 topic echo /sportmodestate --once
ros2 topic hz /sportmodestate

    /sportmodestate is a useful high-level motion-state topic in Unitree's Go2 ROS 2 package. A changing rate from ros2 topic hz confirms that live state messages are arriving without sending a motion command.

    Record a short bag¶

    Record data only after you have confirmed the required topics and have enough space in your laptop's home directory:

    mkdir -p "$HOME/rosbags"
ros2 bag record -o "$HOME/rosbags/go2-state-$(date +%F-%H%M%S)" /sportmodestate

    Press Ctrl+C to finish recording cleanly. Add other topics only after you have checked their names and data rate; image and point-cloud topics can use storage very quickly.

    Standing, sport mode, teleoperation, and custom control¶

    Unitree supplies Go2 examples including go2_stand_example and go2_sport_client. These can command physical movement. They are not suitable as an unsupervised first test and are intentionally not presented here as copy-and-run commands. Before an authorised motion test, confirm all of the following:

    • no app, remote, or other program is issuing competing commands;
    • the robot has a clear, level test area;
    • an observer is present and can stop the robot;
    • the experiment owner has reviewed the exact source code and expected motion;
    • the robot is not charging and its battery level is sufficient.

    Switching off Go2¶

    Source: Unitree's Go2 User Manual, Switching off Go2, printed page 17 (PDF page 15).

    1. Bring Go2 to a stationary standing position on flat ground, with its body level, before requesting the lying posture.
    2. Use the connected app/controller to put Go2 into the prone (lying-down) state. Wait until it has finished lowering itself and is resting on the ground.
    3. Briefly press the battery power button once, then press it again and hold for two seconds to switch off.
    4. Once powered off, arrange the legs and hip joints as shown in the startup placement instructions, ready for the next session. For long-term storage, remove the battery and follow the manual's packing instructions for the transport case.

    Lie down before switching off

    Unitree specifies the prone/damping state before power-off. Cutting power while Go2 is standing can make it fall heavily and damage the robot.

    For a research session, also stop experiment programs and finish any ROS 2 bag recording cleanly. Hand control back to the chosen app/controller before requesting the lying posture. These software handover steps are part of the proposed lab workflow; the exact control method still needs to be confirmed on our robot.

    When something is wrong¶

    • If Go2 is moving unexpectedly, use the lab's designated stop procedure, keep clear, and do not start another control program.
    • If it does not stand, has a red indicator, or shows an abnormal posture, power down only once it is safe and report the fault. Do not recalibrate joints as a first response.
    • If ROS 2 cannot see live topics, check the approved network connection and DDS setup script before changing any configuration.
    • Do not use factory reset to solve an experiment problem.
